# The Status Peak-day range looks forward

The line under the Status screen's next-period prediction reports where the monitor Peak fell across
the last six completed cycles. It was deliberately retrospective — written as a statement about
history — and a test enforced that: the line was barred from carrying _predicted_, _confirmed_ or
_ovulation_, any countdown, and any single unhedged day, so it could never read as a promise about
the current cycle's Peak.

From issue #56 the line looks forward instead:

> Expect your next Peak around cycle day 14 to 16, based on your last 6 completed cycles.

The guard was **rewritten, not deleted**. It was blocking a word rather than the thing it was
actually written to prevent, so the replacement keeps what that reasoning protects — no countdown,
no disclaimer, and no specific unhedged day — and drops the ban on forward-looking phrasing. When
every one of the six cycles peaked on the same day, the line says "around cycle day 15" rather than
naming a date, so the range form survives even when the underlying data is a single point.

## Why the original reasoning still holds

A wrong predicted Peak day costs more than a wrong predicted period start. Peak is the one day the
whole Marquette method hangs on, so a prediction that misses it can leave the user distrusting their
own correct reading. The forward framing is worth that risk only because it is hedged and always a
range. That is the reason the range form was kept rather than dropped for a bare day estimate.

This ADR exists because it overrides a prior deliberate decision. If the guard's new form looks
permissive to you, this is the record of why.
